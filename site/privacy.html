Privacy policy

Effective 26 November 2026 · Covers version {{VERSION}} of the Power Loader extension and this website

We receive nothing. Power Loader has no servers of its own and no account system. It has no analytics, telemetry, tracking, advertising or crash reporting. No case data, page content, settings, keys or usage information is ever sent to Surprisingly Ltd.

Case data stays on your computer. It goes to an AI only if your organisation has set up its own AI gateway, and only when you press an ✦ button, read the preview and approve it. The only other request that can carry a case value is a flow’s fetch step: when you press its button, it asks the casework system you’re using for some of its own pages, and their addresses can contain a value such as the case reference.

This policy is written to meet the transparency requirements of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the UK GDPR. It covers two things published by Surprisingly Limited (“we”, “us”), a company registered in England and Wales, company number 13766094:

  • the Power Loader extension for Firefox, and
  • this website, {{HOST}}, including the demo pages, the demo forms library and the Transfer Bay 07 game.

Who is responsible for what

For the data you handle with the extension, your organisation is the data controller. It installs Power Loader for its staff and decides which systems it may read, where its forms library and AI gateway are, and how long the activity log is kept. Its own privacy notice applies to that data, as do its contracts with any AI provider behind its gateway.

Your organisation also decides what the extension may send, and where. Power Loader can run with no AI at all. If your organisation uses AI, requests go only to the gateway it chooses, after the on-device privacy model (a privacy shield that runs on your computer, and on by default) and the preview. Your organisation can lock these choices with Firefox policy, and its forms library can set some of them. How it works lists them.

Surprisingly Ltd is neither a controller nor a processor of that data. We write and publish the software, but it never sends us anything, and we have no access to any computer it runs on.

For this website and for messages you send us, Surprisingly Ltd is the controller. That covers the small amount of personal data described under This website and If you contact us.

The extension: what it keeps on your computer

Power Loader uses Firefox’s extension storage on the computer where it runs. Nothing in this list is sent anywhere, except the gateway key, which goes only to your organisation’s gateway to sign in each approved request.

  • The active case: the flow it started from, and the values you clipped from pages, each with where it came from. For a page a fetch step read, it also keeps the text around each value it read (at most 500 characters a value), so you can see where the value came from; not the whole page. Depending on the case, these can include names, dates of birth, addresses, reference numbers such as NI numbers and client IDs, and financial, housing, employment and health details.
  • Your notes on the case.
  • An activity log of the approved pages you used and what you did with them, without the values themselves. Pages outside your organisation’s approved list are not logged. Your organisation sets how long entries are kept: 90 days by default.
  • Flows and drafts you make: page layouts, field names and test results, never values from pages. Power Loader checks for and blocks personal data left in a flow before you export it.
  • Settings, and the AI gateway key if you choose Remember the key in this browser (otherwise the key lasts only for the browser session).
  • Cached files from your forms library and the on-device models: the privacy model, and the profile assistant if it has been used to make a profile.

When you finish or discard a case, Power Loader deletes the case, its values and its working data. By default it also deletes a case that is still open when Firefox closes (at the next start) or that has been left for 24 hours without use; you can change both in Settings, unless your organisation has set them. If Firefox closes part-way through a deletion, it completes the next time it starts. Removing the extension removes everything it stored.

Reading text from PDFs and pictures, finding personal data with the privacy model, and suggesting fields with the profile assistant all run entirely on your computer.

The privacy model is on by default. Your organisation, its forms library or you (in Settings) can switch it off. Off, it isn’t downloaded and nothing is scanned with it, but values in the case and the library’s patterns are still redacted before anything goes to an AI, and the preview says the model is off.

Power Loader also works in your browser for you: it reads the pages you clip from, and fills form boxes you have reviewed in a fill plan. It never submits a form. Submitting is always your action, under the terms of the site you submit to.

Things that happen only when you ask

  • Copy diagnostics puts a report on your clipboard for IT support. It contains version numbers, which settings are set and recent error types, never case values, page addresses, notes or keys. It goes nowhere unless you paste it somewhere.
  • Export (flows, activity log, settings) saves a file on your computer.
  • Copying a value puts it on your clipboard.
  • An email step opens your own email app with the message filled in. Power Loader doesn’t send it.

Firefox’s data permissions

When you install Power Loader, Firefox lists the kinds of data it handles: website content, personal information, financial and payment information, and health information. Firefox asks every add-on to declare this. It describes what Power Loader handles on your computer while you work on cases, not anything sent to us.

Automated decisions

Power Loader makes no decisions about people. AI suggestions are shown to you to check, every value is reviewed in the fill plan before it goes into a form, and only you submit.

This website

  • No cookies, no analytics, no tracking. The site sets no cookies. It loads no third-party scripts, fonts or images, and has no embedded content from other sites. The only thing it stores in your browser is whether you muted the game’s sound, in local storage on your own device. You can clear it with your browser’s site data settings.
  • Hosting. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages by GitHub, Inc. When you visit, GitHub receives your IP address and standard request details (browser, address requested, time) and may log them for security and to keep the service running. We don’t receive or look at these logs. See GitHub’s privacy statement.
  • Lawful basis. That processing is in our legitimate interest, and yours, in serving and securing the website (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Transfers outside the UK and EU. GitHub may process this data in the United States. GitHub is certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK extension, and also relies on standard contractual clauses.
  • The demo pages are fictional, and they don’t store or forward anything you type. But submitting the demo Discretionary housing payment form opens its confirmation page with the form’s answers in the page address, so, like any page request, that address reaches our host and may appear in its logs. Use only made-up details in the demo.

If you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address, name and whatever you write. We use it only to reply and to deal with your request. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in answering you (Article 6(1)(f)). We don’t share it with anyone, and we delete it once the conversation is finished, normally within 12 months. Please don’t send us personal data about people in your cases.

If you report an issue on GitHub, it is public and covered by GitHub’s terms. Use Copy diagnostics, which contains no case data, rather than screenshots of real cases.

Your rights

Under the GDPR and UK GDPR you have the right to access your personal data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to its processing, and to data portability.

  • About data in the extension (yours, or that of people in your cases): contact your organisation’s data protection officer. We hold none of it, so we can’t access, correct or delete it. You can delete everything on your computer by finishing or discarding the case, or by removing the extension.
  • About emails you’ve sent us: write to privacy@surprisingly.ltd. We’ll reply within one month.
  • About website hosting logs: these are held by GitHub. Contact GitHub through its privacy statement. We can help if you ask.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office; in the EU, the authority in the country where you live or work (see the list of EU data protection authorities). We’d appreciate the chance to sort it out first.
